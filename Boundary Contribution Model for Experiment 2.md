# Boundary Contribution Model for Experiment 2

## Status and scope

This analysis uses the complete set of 495 measured step-2,000, (k=4)
mixed-width partitions. It asks whether the relative damage caused by a frozen
mixed-width intervention can be predicted from the identities of the removed
H16 boundaries.

For every partition (P), retain the registered response:

\[
\Delta NLL(P)=NLL(P)-NLL(\text{native H16}).
\]

Lower is better. Every measured (k=4) partition has positive \(\Delta NLL\),
so no boundary may be described as beneficial relative to native H16. Boundary
scores are relative desirability scores within the frozen (k=4) population.
They do not establish the performance of a model trained with another routing
granularity.

## 1. Candidate boundaries and observations

There are 16 atomic 80-dimensional H16 chunks and 15 adjacent boundaries:

\[
e_i=(h_i,h_{i+1}),\qquad i=0,\ldots,14.
\]

Represent a partition by indicators

\[
x_i(P)=\mathbf 1[e_i\text{ is removed}].
\]

Every observation contains exactly four non-overlapping removals:

\[
\sum_{i=0}^{14}x_i(P)=4.
\]

The complete valid design contains

\[
\binom{16-4}{4}=495
\]

partitions.

## 2. Primary constrained additive model

The intercept and the 15 raw indicators are collinear because every row has
exactly four selected boundaries. Use centered indicators

\[
p_i=\frac{1}{495}\sum_Px_i(P),\qquad z_i(P)=x_i(P)-p_i,
\]

and fit the identified model

\[
\boxed{
\Delta NLL(P)=\mu+\sum_{i=0}^{14}\beta_i z_i(P)+\epsilon_P,
\qquad \sum_{i=0}^{14}\beta_i=0.
}
\]

The sum-to-zero constraint is part of the estimand and must be enforced in all
fits and cross-validation folds.

Interpretation:

- \(\mu\) is the mean (k=4) \(\Delta NLL\).
- \(\beta_i<0\) means boundary (i) is less damaging than the average boundary
  removal at (k=4).
- \(\beta_i>0\) means boundary (i) is more damaging than average.
- Coefficients are descriptive, not causal.

Do not describe a negative coefficient as an improvement over native H16.

## 3. Additive-model validation

Use deterministic shuffled five-fold cross-validation over the 495 partitions.
Fit all centering values and coefficients inside each training fold. Preserve
continuous \(\Delta NLL\); never replace it with ranks or class labels.

Report out-of-fold:

- \(R^2\);
- RMSE;
- MAE;
- Spearman correlation between predicted and measured \(\Delta NLL\).

Also report the constant-mean baseline. Save every out-of-fold prediction and
the exact fold seed.

The current fixed-seed diagnostic is:

\[
R^2_{\mathrm{CV}}\approx0.933,\qquad
\rho_{\mathrm{Spearman}}\approx0.971.
\]

These values must be regenerated by the checked-in implementation rather than
copied into final results.

## 4. Boundary score deliverable

Fit the constrained additive model on all 495 observations and produce one row
per boundary containing:

- boundary index and adjacent pair;
- \(\beta_i\);
- relative desirability rank;
- frequency in all 495 partitions;
- frequency in the top 10, top 20, and top 50 measured partitions;
- mean measured \(\Delta NLL\) with and without the boundary, explicitly labeled
  as a coupled descriptive association.

Sort the primary score table by \(\beta_i\), lowest first. The expected leading
ordering from the diagnostic fit is

\[
(6,7),\ (5,6),\ (14,15),\ (13,14).
\]

The second and fourth entries are scientifically useful because simple
top-frequency counts suppress boundaries that overlap with a slightly stronger
neighbor.

Do not report ordinary coefficient confidence intervals. The 495 partitions
exhaust the (k=4) combinatorial population, and per-sequence discovery losses
were not retained for every partition. Partition resampling may be reported only
as surrogate-fit stability, not language-model evaluation-data uncertainty.

## 5. Pairwise model: prediction only

For compatible boundaries that can co-occur, construct pair indicators

\[
q_{ij}=x_ix_j.
\]

There are 91 observable compatible pairs. At fixed (k=4),

\[
\sum_{j\text{ compatible with }i}x_ix_j=3x_i.
\]

Therefore main and pair terms are structurally aliased. The nominal
intercept-plus-main-plus-pair design has 107 columns but rank 91.

Fit a standardized ridge model with main and compatible-pair features only as a
within-(k=4) predictive diagnostic. Select the ridge penalty by nested
cross-validation and report out-of-fold \(R^2\), RMSE, MAE, and Spearman.

The current fixed-seed diagnostic is approximately

\[
R^2_{\mathrm{CV}}=0.998,\qquad \rho_{\mathrm{Spearman}}=0.999.
\]

Do not interpret or publish individual ridge interaction coefficients as unique
synergies. Their values depend on feature scaling, regularization, and the
chosen parameterization. Do not transfer this pairwise model to another (k).

## 6. Additive ranking for other merge counts

Only the constrained additive boundary scores are eligible for the first
cross-(k) transfer test. For a valid candidate (P), define

\[
\boxed{S(P)=\sum_{i\in P}\beta_i.}
\]

Lower \(S(P)\) predicts a less damaging partition within the same (k). Do not
use the (k=4) intercept to predict absolute \(\Delta NLL\) at another (k), and
do not compare scores across different (k) values.

First rank all valid candidates at

\[
k=3:\ 286\text{ candidates},\qquad
k=5:\ 462\text{ candidates}.
\]

The pure (k=8) H8 endpoint has only one candidate and should be retained as a
later architectural/frozen-intervention anchor, not treated as a ranking task.

## 7. Frozen transfer selections

Before evaluating any (k=3) or (k=5) candidate, write immutable selection
manifests containing the source discovery-results SHA-256, fitted-score-table
SHA-256, code commit, seed, partition IDs, predicted ranks, scores, and roles.

Use two logically separate samples per (k).

### 7.1 Targeted search-performance sample

Select:

- predicted top 10;
- predicted middle 5;
- predicted bottom 5.

This sample tests whether the model concentrates less damaging candidates near
the top. It is not used to estimate population-wide transfer correlation.

### 7.2 Frozen uniform transfer sample

Using the registered seed, sample 30 candidates uniformly without replacement
from the complete valid candidate set for that (k). Do not exclude targeted
candidates before sampling. If a uniform draw overlaps a targeted candidate,
retain both roles and evaluate that partition once; resampling would destroy the
uniform design.

This sample estimates transfer Spearman correlation. Keep its candidate IDs
frozen even if preliminary targeted results are disappointing.

All candidates must satisfy the non-overlapping adjacent-merge constraint.

## 8. Transfer evaluation

Evaluate the frozen (k=3) and (k=5) manifests on:

- the immutable step-2,000 H16 checkpoint;
- the existing fixed untouched confirmation split;
- bf16, batch size 1;
- the same global partition at all 73 routing sites;
- native H16 reevaluated in each run as the paired baseline.

Do not resume training while this transfer experiment is running.

For every evaluated candidate save total NLL, \(\Delta NLL\) versus that run's
native H16 baseline, per-sequence losses, elapsed time, predicted score, predicted
rank, and sampling roles.

## 9. Transfer analysis

For the frozen uniform sample, report separately for (k=3) and (k=5):

- Spearman correlation between \(S(P)\) and actual \(\Delta NLL\);
- RMSE and MAE after fitting only an affine calibration on the uniform sample,
  clearly labeled as descriptive rather than preregistered prediction error;
- a scatter plot with every uniform candidate labeled by predicted rank.

For the targeted sample report:

- mean and median actual \(\Delta NLL\) for top, middle, and bottom strata;
- top-versus-middle and top-versus-bottom separation;
- rank of each targeted candidate within the evaluated set.

Do not combine the targeted and uniform samples when estimating transfer
Spearman. Overlapping candidates retain both roles but enter the uniform
calculation once.

## 10. Decision rule

Treat transfer as promising for a given (k) when both conditions hold:

1. Uniform-sample Spearman is positive and materially large.
2. Predicted-top candidates have lower actual \(\Delta NLL\) than the middle and
   bottom targeted strata.

Do not define a threshold after seeing results. Report the exact estimates and
candidate-level table even if transfer is weak or reversed.

Only if the (k=4\rightarrow k=3/5) transfer is successful should additive
scores be used to shrink searches for (k=1,2,6,7).

## Core interpretation boundary

The (k=4) analysis may establish that variation among frozen mixed-width
partitions is strongly structured by boundary identity, with secondary
predictable combinatorial structure. The transfer test may establish whether
those relative boundary scores rank frozen (k=3/5) interventions.

Neither result establishes that a mixed-width or H8 architecture trained from
scratch would outperform native H16. That requires a separate training
experiment.
